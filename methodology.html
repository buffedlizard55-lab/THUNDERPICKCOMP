Core values — our decision filter

Maximize P(Win) — “Maximize the Probability of Winning”: in every tradeoff we pick the path that maximizes this project's chance of becoming a genuinely useful, trustworthy daily hub. We set aside emotion and put the project first.

Own the Outcome — we own results end to end, not just our slice. When we can act, we do — without waiting for permission. Failures and successes are signals to improve. At every turn we stay accountable to the final outcome.

These are the focal point when building, researching, suggesting upgrades, and implementing work — per the README charter. The mission is to solve the manual-checking problem: a current, traceable daily feed so users don't have to manually track HLTV, Liquipedia, team feeds and markets.

Verification rules (non-negotiable)

  1. No hallucinations. Teams, rosters, odds, prices, results, stats, dates — if it can't be checked against a source, it's unknown/TBD.
  2. Claim by claim. Every material statement links to the source it was checked against, with a retrieval timestamp. A search snippet is not verification.
  3. Primary sources first: organizer releases, teams/players, Valve, exchange APIs — then HLTV/Liquipedia structured records, then reputable press with attribution.
  4. Fact vs analysis. Verified facts, calculations, model heuristics and simulated outputs are labeled as such. Strategy theses are testable rules, not punditry.
  5. Free public sources only. No paid API or subscription. Source terms, rate limits and attribution requirements are respected.
  6. Fail visibly. Missing feeds, stale prices and conflicts are shown as flags — never smoothed over.

Settlement, source scope & reproducibility

  • What is automatically collected: Valve's latest published Global VRS file (ranked rosters, not live event starters); a conservative Liquipedia fixture-template signal; bounded Polymarket event searches and outcome-specific CLOB best asks; bounded Kalshi CS2 game/outright series and first-party Yes asks. Each query's scope, timestamp and failure are published on Markets.
  • What is simulated: only the FURIA/Falcons outright policy can create two atomic paper decisions from fresh, adequately sized best asks before the Finals. It does not claim a real fill; the six match strategies are paused and the control account never bets.
  • Gross math: a $1-winning share at ask p has modeled decimal factor 1/p; on confirmed win the gross paper payout is stake/p rounded to cents. Real fees, slippage, order size and tax are not modeled. Settled P/L is payout − stake; pending stakes are locked, with no realized ROI.
  • Settlement: not yet automated. Require the venue’s actual resolution and independent HLTV/Liquipedia result checks. A canceled/forfeited match may resolve at fair-market-price or 50/50 rather than refund; do not assume a void. Pending entries stay pending until an immutable settlement journal is built.
  • scripts/validate.py checks schemas, provenance, references, duplicate IDs and gross math. Offline unit tests use explicitly partial captured source excerpts and synthetic decision cases that never enter published data.
